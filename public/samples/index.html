PWIN · 79 PRE-RACE FEATURES

A whole-field estimate, built horse by horse.

PWIN estimates each runner’s chance of winning. It combines 79 pre-race features—built from race-card and past-performance data, condition fit, pace, recency and verified connections—then evaluates every horse against today’s field.

→

→
PWINestimated chance for each runner
→
Race Cardprojected-win order and fair odds
01
Speed and form

Recent, best and 90-day speed evidence; same-surface and similar-distance performance; field-relative strength; trends, variability and how much history supports the estimate.

02
Surface and distance fit

Today’s surface, route or sprint, and distance are compared with relevant past starts. The model accounts for similarity, sample size, stretch-outs, cutbacks and sprint-to-route or route-to-sprint changes.

03
Expected pace and matchup

Early and late pace signals are read together, with surface and distance interactions. Pace matters in context: early speed is not an automatic bonus, and late strength depends on the race shape.

04
Trainer and jockey context

Source-checked trainer, jockey and partnership ratings are paired with their supporting sample sizes and win or in-the-money records. Known, limited and unavailable evidence is kept distinct.

05
Recency, development and reliability

Age and development status, days since the last race, freshness of the evidence, relevant-start counts, figure variability and uncertainty help distinguish a strong signal from a thin record.

06
The field itself

Field size and each runner’s performance relative to the other entrants matter. PWIN is a race-level distribution, not a reusable rating that ignores who else is entered.

How to read the order: runners are listed by projected-win rank; saddlecloth numbers identify the horses, not their rank. WIN% is each runner’s estimated share of the win probability, normalized across the active field (about 100% total after display rounding). Fair Odds translate that estimate into an odds reference. A projected rank is a comparison, not a promised finishing order.

Independent PWIN: the morning line and current market odds are not model inputs. PWIN is built from evidence available before the race. Historical results measure how prior estimates performed; the outcome of the race being predicted cannot enter its own probability.

Selected runners, with a reason at the price.

03FAVORITE & ALTERNATIVE

A closer look when the favorite can be challenged.

The Target List focuses on races where the morning-line favorite looks vulnerable and a specific alternative has a stronger, evidence-backed case. Each matchup shows both sides so the reasoning is clear.

  1. 1

    Race conditions

    Track, race, post time, surface and distance frame the matchup before you get to the analysis.

  2. 2

    Favorite versus alternative

    “ML Favorite” and “Attack” label the two horses, with their morning line, estimated win probability and fair odds shown side by side.

  3. 3

    Why the alternative merits attention

    The written case explains the favorite’s vulnerability and the challenger’s affirmative angle, including why the listed price may be compelling.
